DETAILED MATHEMATICAL EXPLANATION

1) WHAT ARE THE NAVIER–STOKES EQUATIONS?

The Navier–Stokes equations describe the motion of viscous fluids
(liquids and gases).

They express Newton’s second law applied to a fluid element:

    Force = mass × acceleration

Unknowns:

    u(x,t)  = velocity field (vector field)
    p(x,t)  = pressure
    ρ       = density
    ν       = viscosity

For an incompressible fluid (constant density), the equations are:

    ∂u/∂t + (u · ∇)u = −∇p + νΔu
    ∇ · u = 0

The first equation represents conservation of momentum.
The second equation represents incompressibility.

------------------------------------------------------------

2) MEANING OF EACH TERM

(a) ∂u/∂t
    Time change of velocity.

(b) (u · ∇)u
    Nonlinear convection term.
    Represents transport of momentum by the fluid itself.
    This term causes turbulence and mathematical difficulty.

(c) −∇p
    Force due to pressure differences.

(d) νΔu
    Viscous diffusion term.
    Smooths out irregularities in the velocity.

(e) ∇ · u = 0
    No compression: volume is preserved.

------------------------------------------------------------

3) WHY THEY MODEL WAVES AND TURBULENCE

Boat wakes, air turbulence behind jets, ocean currents,
and atmospheric circulation are governed by these equations.

The equations predict:

    - Laminar flow (smooth motion)
    - Turbulent flow (chaotic motion)
    - Vortex formation
    - Energy dissipation

The difficulty lies in the nonlinear term (u · ∇)u.

------------------------------------------------------------

4) THE MATHEMATICAL PROBLEM

We want to answer:

    Given smooth initial velocity u0(x),
    does a smooth solution exist for all time?

More precisely:

    If u0 is smooth and divergence-free,
    does there exist a unique smooth solution u(x,t)
    defined for all t ≥ 0?

------------------------------------------------------------

5) LOCAL VS GLOBAL EXISTENCE

Local existence:
    Smooth solutions exist for small time.
    (This is known and proven.)

Global existence:
    Do solutions remain smooth for all time?

This is unknown in three dimensions.

------------------------------------------------------------

6) POSSIBLE FAILURE: BLOW-UP

A solution "blows up" if:

    ||u(x,t)|| becomes infinite
    in finite time.

This would mean formation of a singularity.

The key open question:

    Can singularities form from smooth initial data?

In 2 dimensions:
    Global smooth solutions are known to exist.

In 3 dimensions:
    The problem is open.

------------------------------------------------------------

7) ENERGY ESTIMATE

Multiply the equation by u and integrate over space.

One obtains:

    d/dt ∫ |u|^2 dx = − 2ν ∫ |∇u|^2 dx

This shows:

    Total kinetic energy decreases over time.

However, this estimate alone does not prevent
possible singularities in 3D.

------------------------------------------------------------

8) THE MILLENNIUM PROBLEM

The challenge is to prove one of the following:

    (A) For every smooth initial condition in R^3,
        there exists a unique global smooth solution.

or

    (B) There exists smooth initial data that leads
        to finite-time blow-up.

This is one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems.

------------------------------------------------------------

9) WHY IT IS SO DIFFICULT

The main obstruction is the nonlinear term:

    (u · ∇)u

It transfers energy between scales.

In three dimensions, vortex stretching can amplify
velocity gradients.

This creates the possibility of singularities.

There is no known method that controls this effect
for all time.

------------------------------------------------------------

10) WHAT PROGRESS WOULD MEAN

A full mathematical theory would:

    - Explain turbulence formation
    - Clarify energy cascade mechanisms
    - Establish precise regularity criteria
    - Provide rigorous global existence or blow-up results

Despite being written in the 19th century,
a complete 3D theory remains unknown.

That is the central mathematical challenge.
