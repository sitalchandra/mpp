DETAILED MATHEMATICAL EXPLANATION (WITH EQUATIONS)

1) FROM NEWTON TO QUANTUM FIELD THEORY

Classical mechanics is governed by Newton’s equation:

    F = m a

or equivalently,

    m d^2x/dt^2 = F(x,t)

Quantum mechanics replaces particle trajectories by wavefunctions ψ(x,t)
satisfying Schrödinger’s equation:

    iħ ∂ψ/∂t = Hψ

Relativistic quantum field theory replaces particles by fields:

    φ(x,t),  Aμ(x,t),  ψ(x,t)

These satisfy field equations derived from a Lagrangian density L.

--------------------------------------------------------------------

2) CLASSICAL YANG–MILLS THEORY

Yang–Mills theory is a non-abelian gauge theory.

Let G be a compact Lie group (e.g., SU(N)).
Let:

    Aμ(x) = gauge field (Lie algebra valued)

The field strength (curvature) is:

    Fμν = ∂μAν − ∂νAμ + [Aμ, Aν]

Compare with electromagnetism (abelian case):

    Fμν = ∂μAν − ∂νAμ

The extra commutator term:

    [Aμ, Aν]

creates nonlinear self-interactions.

The Yang–Mills action is:

    S = (1/4g^2) ∫ tr(Fμν F^μν) d^4x

The Euler–Lagrange equations give:

    Dμ F^μν = 0

where:

    Dμ = ∂μ + [Aμ, ·]

is the covariant derivative.

These are the classical Yang–Mills equations.

--------------------------------------------------------------------

3) GAUGE SYMMETRY

Under a gauge transformation:

    Aμ → g Aμ g^(−1) − (∂μ g) g^(−1)

where g(x) ∈ G.

Physics is invariant under such transformations.

This geometric structure corresponds to:

    Connections on principal G-bundles
    Curvature = Fμν

Thus Yang–Mills theory is deeply geometric.

--------------------------------------------------------------------

4) QUANTUM YANG–MILLS THEORY

To quantize, one formally defines the path integral:

    Z = ∫ exp(iS[A]/ħ) DA

Observables are expectation values:

    ⟨O⟩ = (1/Z) ∫ O[A] exp(iS[A]/ħ) DA

In four dimensions (R^4), the goal is to define this rigorously.

However:

    The measure DA is not mathematically well-defined.
    Renormalization is required.
    Ultraviolet divergences appear.

A full constructive definition is unknown.

--------------------------------------------------------------------

5) MASS GAP

In classical Yang–Mills theory (no mass term),
the equations are scale invariant.

Classical waves travel at speed c,
so the spectrum contains massless excitations.

In quantum theory, one studies the Hamiltonian H.
The mass m of a particle corresponds to:

    H |ψ⟩ = E |ψ⟩
    E^2 = p^2 + m^2

A mass gap means:

    There exists Δ > 0 such that
    Spec(H) ⊆ {0} ∪ [Δ, ∞)

That is:

    The lowest nonzero energy state
    has energy ≥ Δ.

Equivalently:

    No massless excitations exist.

--------------------------------------------------------------------

6) WHY MASS GAP IS SURPRISING

The classical Lagrangian contains no mass term:

    L = (1/4g^2) tr(Fμν F^μν)

Yet quantum effects generate an effective mass scale.

This phenomenon is called:

    Dimensional transmutation.

The coupling constant runs with energy:

    μ dg/dμ = −β(g)

For SU(N):

    β(g) ≈ −(11N/48π^2) g^3 + ...

Since β(g) < 0:

    g(μ) → 0 as μ → ∞
    (asymptotic freedom)

At low energies, the coupling becomes strong,
leading to confinement and a mass gap.

--------------------------------------------------------------------

7) CONFINEMENT AND CORRELATION FUNCTIONS

The mass gap is related to decay of correlation functions.

Let:

    G(x) = ⟨O(x) O(0)⟩

If there is a mass gap m > 0, then:

    G(x) ~ e^(−m|x|)

for large |x|.

Exponential decay corresponds to positive mass.

If no mass gap:

    G(x) ~ power-law decay.

Thus proving a mass gap amounts to proving
exponential decay of correlations.

--------------------------------------------------------------------

8) THE MILLENNIUM PROBLEM

One must prove:

    For any compact simple gauge group G,
    quantum Yang–Mills theory exists on R^4
    and has a mass gap Δ > 0.

More precisely:

    Construct a quantum field theory satisfying
    the Wightman axioms
    and show Spec(H) has a positive gap.

No complete mathematical construction exists.

--------------------------------------------------------------------

9) WHY THIS IS HARD

Difficulties include:

    - Nonlinear self-interaction: [Aμ, Aν]
    - Renormalization in 4D
    - Strong coupling at low energies
    - Non-perturbative effects

Perturbation theory expands:

    exp(iS/ħ) = ∑ (Feynman diagrams)

But this is only formal.

A non-perturbative construction is required.

--------------------------------------------------------------------

10) CENTRAL SIGNIFICANCE

Yang–Mills theory underlies:

    - Quantum chromodynamics (QCD)
    - The Standard Model of particle physics

Physically:

    Experiments show particles (gluons, hadrons)
    have positive mass.

Mathematically:

    Prove existence of a 4D quantum Yang–Mills theory
    with a strictly positive mass gap.

That remains an open fundamental problem.
